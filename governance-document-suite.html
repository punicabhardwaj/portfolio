Anyone can list frameworks. This suite is the paper spine of a governance program I designed end to end: the decision rights, the gates, the documents, and the triggers that connect them. The documents cross-reference each other the way a real governance program does. The inventory feeds the risk assessment. The risk assessment cites policy controls. The model card operationalizes fairness thresholds. The incident response plan defines what happens when monitoring in the model card trips a limit.

This inventory is the authoritative register of every AI and machine learning system developed, procured, or operated by Evergreen Mutual Bank and its EU subsidiary (Evergreen Europe DAC, Dublin). 1.0Executive summary

CreditSense v3.2 automates approve/decline/refer decisions and line assignment for unsecured consumer credit. It is the bank's highest-exposure AI system: roughly 38,000 decisions per month, direct effect on customers' access to credit, ECOA/Regulation B adverse action obligations in the US, and EU AI Act high-risk obligations through Evergreen Europe DAC. Aggregate inherent risk is rated High, anchored by R-01 at the matrix maximum (25/25). With the control set described in section 5, the highest residual score is 10/25 (Medium), within the risk appetite the Board approved for Tier 1 lending models, subject to three conditions tracked in section 6.

2.0Scope and methodology

The assessment covers the production scoring pipeline, the policy-rule overlay, the adverse action reason-code generator, upstream credit bureau and bank-internal data feeds, and the human refer-queue workflow. It excludes collections strategy models (assessed separately as AIS-005 adjacent scope).

  • Framing: NIST AI RMF MAP function workshops with first-line owners, compliance, fair lending, and data engineering (MAP 1.1, 1.5, 3.4).
  • Measurement: quantitative testing per the MEASURE function: discrimination testing, stability analysis, challenger benchmarking, and adversarial review of the application channel (MEASURE 2.6, 2.11).
  • Scoring: 5×5 likelihood × impact scale; inherent scored without controls, residual scored with verified controls only. Verification by second-line MRM consistent with SR 11-7 effective challenge.
  • EU overlay: Article 9 risk management, Article 10 data governance, Article 13 transparency, Article 14 human oversight requirements mapped for the EU deployment.

5.0Control environment summary

Controls cluster into four layers, each owned by a named function and each testable by Internal Audit:

  1. Pre-deployment gates: intake classification, MRM independent validation, fairness audit, and fair lending legal sign-off. No gate may be waived for Tier 1 systems (POL-AI-001 §7.3).
  2. Runtime monitoring: drift (PSI), performance by segment, fairness metrics, override rates, and reason-code fidelity, with thresholds wired to the incident plan (IRP-AI-001 §3).
  3. Human oversight: a staffed refer queue with measured effectiveness, not assumed effectiveness; analysts can decline to follow the model and their dissent is a tracked signal.
  4. Change and documentation discipline: versioned models, regenerated model cards, and an EU technical file that updates with the system rather than after it.

1.0Purpose

This policy establishes how Evergreen Mutual Bank develops, procures, deploys, monitors, and retires artificial intelligence so that AI use remains within Board-approved risk appetite, complies with applicable law, and is defensible to regulators, auditors, and customers. It implements an AI management system consistent with ISO/IEC 42001 and operationalizes the NIST AI RMF GOVERN function.

2.0Scope and definitions

In scope: any system that uses machine learning, statistical learning, or generative models to produce predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions affecting the bank, its customers, or its employees, whether built, bought, or embedded in vendor products. Embedded AI features in procured software are in scope at the moment they are enabled.

AI system: defined per EU AI Act Article 3(1) for EU operations and per this policy's functional definition elsewhere. Consequential decision: a decision materially affecting a person's access to credit, employment, accounts, or funds. Generative AI: models producing text, code, images, or audio, including third-party assistants.

3.0Principles

  1. Accountability is human. Every AI system has one named accountable executive owner. "The model decided" is never an accepted explanation.
  2. Proportionality. Control depth scales with the risk tier defined in §5, so low-risk uses move fast and high-risk uses move carefully.
  3. No unmonitored consequential decisions. Tier 1 systems require measured, effective human oversight, not nominal oversight.
  4. Evidence over assertion. Fairness, performance, and compliance claims must be supported by retained, reproducible test artifacts.
  5. Transparency to affected people. Customers are told when they interact with AI and receive meaningful, specific reasons for adverse decisions.

All systems are tiered at intake using the criteria in EMB-GOV-INV-001 §2.0 and re-tiered on any material change of purpose, population, or autonomy. EU AI Act classification is recorded alongside the internal tier; where the two diverge, the stricter control set applies. §5.1: a system absent from the inventory is an unauthorized system and is subject to immediate shutdown.

7.0Specific requirements

7.1 Prohibited uses

  • Social scoring of customers or employees; emotion inference in the workplace or in collections; biometric categorization by protected attributes (aligned to EU AI Act Article 5, applied bank-wide, not only in the EU).
  • Use of protected-class attributes, or knowing proxies for them, as decision features in consequential decisions, except as required for fairness testing itself.
  • Fully automated account closure or credit denial without a defined human appeal path.

7.2 Generative AI acceptable use

  • Only bank-approved GenAI tools may touch bank information; confidential or customer data may be entered only into tools on the approved register with enterprise data protections verified.
  • GenAI output used in customer communications, code, or analysis requires human review proportionate to impact; the reviewer is accountable for the output.
  • Customer-facing GenAI must disclose that it is AI (EU AI Act Art. 50) and must have an escalation path to a human.

7.3 Waivers and stop conditions

Tier 2 and Tier 3 gate waivers may be granted by the AIGC with documented rationale and expiry. Tier 1 gates may not be waived. Any verified fairness floor breach, regulatory notice, or SEV-1 incident invokes the stop conditions in IRP-AI-001, including suspension of automated decisioning, regardless of business impact.

7.4 Training and attestation

Annual role-based training: all staff (awareness), system owners and developers (lifecycle obligations), AIGC members (regulatory horizon). Annual acceptable-use attestation is a condition of system access.

1.0Model details

TaskBinary default-risk classification (24-month horizon) with calibrated probability output, mapped to approve / refer / decline bands and initial line assignment by a separate policy-rule overlay.
ArchitectureGradient-boosted decision tree ensemble (1,200 trees, max depth 6), monotonic constraints applied to 14 financially intuitive features (e.g., risk may not decrease as delinquency count increases).
Features87 inputs: bureau tradeline aggregates, inquiry velocity, internal deposit relationship signals, application attributes. No protected-class attributes. Three geography-derived features under constraint review (RA condition C-2).
Developed byIn-house Data Science, Consumer Lending pod. Training pipeline version-controlled; model registry entry reg://creditsense/3.2.0.
Decision authorityAuto-approve and auto-decline within score bands; mid-band routes to human refer queue (≈ 18% of volume). Appeal path available for all declines.

2.0Intended use and out-of-scope use

Intended: initial credit decisioning for unsecured personal loans and credit cards for applicants aged 18+ in US and EU (Ireland) markets, on application volumes resembling the training population.

  • Out of scope: credit line increases on existing accounts (separate model), small business lending, collections treatment, employment or tenant screening of any kind, and marketing eligibility.
  • Known unsuitability: thin-file applicants with fewer than two tradelines receive degraded discrimination (section 4); these route to the refer queue by policy rule, not by model confidence alone.

3.0Training data

  • Corpus: 1.42M historical applications, 2019-01 through 2024-09, with 24-month performance outcomes. Synthetic data is not used.
  • Exclusions: COVID forbearance-period outcomes (2020-04 to 2021-06) reweighted to avoid learning emergency-policy artifacts; fraud-confirmed applications removed; withdrawn applications excluded from the label set.
  • Reject inference: performance of declined applicants estimated via bureau-observed proxy outcomes; method and its limits documented in the validation report.
  • Data governance: sources, lineage, retention, and quality thresholds recorded in the catalog and the EU AI Act Article 10 data file. Protected-class data used only in segregated fairness-testing storage with access controls.

6.0Explainability and adverse action

Per-decision SHAP attributions are computed at scoring time and mapped to a controlled vocabulary of 28 Reg B-compatible reason codes. The top four negative contributors populate the adverse action notice. A fidelity test in the release pipeline verifies that stated reasons track actual score drivers (R-09 control). Top decline reasons in production: serious delinquency history, high revolving utilization, insufficient credit history length, recent inquiry velocity.

7.0Monitoring and retraining

  • Drift: PSI on top-20 features; alert > 0.10, action > 0.25 with auto-escalation to IRP-AI-001 playbook PB-3.

  • Fairness: quarterly audit as above; any sub-0.80 DIR result invokes playbook PB-2 same day.
  • Retraining: scheduled annually or on action-level drift; every retrain repeats the full Tier 1 gate sequence in POL-AI-001 §6. No silent refreshes.

1.0Purpose and relationship to enterprise IR

AI systems fail differently from conventional software: they can be fully operational and still cause harm through biased, drifting, fabricated, or manipulated outputs. This plan defines what counts as an AI incident, how severity is assigned, who has authority to suspend a system, and which regulatory clocks start when. It is an annex to the enterprise incident response plan; security incidents with an AI dimension run under the CISO with this plan's AI workstream attached.

4.0Roles and suspension authority

  • Incident Commander (IC): AI Risk Manager or delegate; runs the bridge, owns the timeline record, decides severity changes.
  • Suspension authority: the IC, the CRO, the system owner, or the on-call MRM lead may each unilaterally suspend automated decisioning or trigger an agent kill switch. No one may veto a suspension; only the AIGC may authorize reactivation. Asymmetry is deliberate: turning a system off requires one person, turning it back on requires the committee.
  • Workstreams: Communications (customer + internal), Legal/Regulatory (notification clocks, privilege), Engineering (containment, forensics, rollback), Remediation (affected-customer identification and make-whole).

5.0Response lifecycle

  1. Detect: monitoring thresholds (drift, fairness, fidelity), complaint-pattern surveillance, staff reporting channel, vendor notifications. Every Tier 1 monitoring breach auto-opens a ticket; humans triage, machines never close.
  2. Triage and classify: severity per §3; preserve model version, feature snapshots, decision logs, and prompts/outputs before any change (evidence first, fixes second).
  3. Contain: options in order of preference: route affected segment to human decisioning, tighten policy-rule overlay, suspend automated decisioning, full system suspension / kill switch. Containment for decisioning systems means stopping new harm while keeping customers served by humans.
  4. Investigate: root cause across data, model, deployment, and oversight layers; MRM joins for any Tier 1 system.
  5. Remediate and make whole: fix root cause; re-run affected decisions where feasible; for credit incidents, re-decision harmed applicants and correct bureau furnishing.
  6. Notify: per §6 clocks; customer notification drafted with Legal but written in plain language.
  7. Post-incident review: blameless PIR within 10 business days for SEV-1/2; actions tracked to closure by the AIGC; this plan and relevant model cards updated (ISO 42001 Cl. 10).
